What is terminal velocity?

By Ines Duarte · 4 October 2026 · 6 min read

Terminal velocity is the constant speed a falling object reaches when air resistance balances its weight. The object stops accelerating at that point.

Skydivers

A belly-down skydiver reaches a terminal velocity of about 55 m/s (200 km/h). Pulling into a head-down position can raise that to roughly 90 m/s.

A small steel ball has a much higher terminal velocity than a feather because its weight is large compared with the drag it experiences.

Why shape matters

Drag depends on the cross-sectional area facing the air and on a drag coefficient that captures how streamlined the body is. A flat, spread-out body presents a large area and falls slowly; a narrow, pointed body falls much faster.

Air density also changes the answer. In thinner air at high altitude the same body falls faster, which is why a jump from very high up begins with extreme speeds before the atmosphere thickens.

  • Parachute open: a few metres per second
  • Cat in a curled posture: slower than most small animals expect
  • Raindrop: roughly 9 m/s depending on size
