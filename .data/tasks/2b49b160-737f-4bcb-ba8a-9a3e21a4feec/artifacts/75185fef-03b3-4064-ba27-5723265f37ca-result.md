A database index is an auxiliary data structure—commonly a B-tree or hash table—that stores a sorted or hashed subset of a table’s columns so the database can locate matching rows without scanning the entire table. It works like a book’s index: instead of reading every page, the engine jumps close to the relevant data. Indexes speed up reads, filters, joins, and sorting, but they consume storage and add overhead to inserts, updates, and deletes, so they are best used on columns involved in frequent lookups.
